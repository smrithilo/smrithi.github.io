About

I am a second-year Robotics master’s student at the University of Pennsylvania, graduating in May 2027. My research interests are in robot learning for manipulation, world models, and adaptation from physical interaction. I want to understand what robots must represent to predict the consequences of their actions, recognize when their expectations are wrong, and learn from unexpected outcomes.

At Penn’s GRASP Lab, I work with Prof. Antonio Loquercio on bimanual manipulation for chemistry workflows, collecting demonstrations and training, fine-tuning, and deploying visuomotor diffusion policies and Large Behavior Models on real robots. Watching these policies fail after a missed contact or a small change in the scene has shaped the questions I now want to investigate.

With Prof. Jiayuan Mao, I am exploring whether successful and failed manipulation videos can help reconstruct simulators that predict physical outcomes under new actions. In parallel, my PrismaX capstone studies post-training adaptation of pretrained vision–language–action policies as tasks and environments change. Both projects are in progress. My broader goal is to connect predictive representations with failure detection, informative interaction, and recovery in long-horizon manipulation.

My path into robotics began with student robotics teams and research on cable-driven continuum robots with Prof. Ashwin K. P. I later worked on hierarchical planning for multi-robot collective construction with Prof. Howie Choset at Carnegie Mellon’s Biorobotics Lab, and built ROS 2 autonomy infrastructure at Open Science Stack.

I am seeking part-time robotics, AI, and ML opportunities for Spring 2027 and full-time opportunities for Summer 2027.
